## Core Non-Negotiable Rules

Use these rules in every version:

- The source file may be in **English**.
- The final output must be a **structured bilingual mix**.
- **Technical terms, headings, scientific names, formulas, and important lecture vocabulary should stay in English exactly as they appear in the source whenever appropriate.**
- **Explanations, simplifications, clarifications, and memory-friendly comments should be written in clear Egyptian Arabic colloquial style.**
- All information must remain **strictly faithful to the uploaded file**.
- Do **not** omit essential ideas.
- Do **not** add facts that are not present in the source.
- The result must look like **real handwritten study notes**.
- The layout must support **fast review and exam preparation**.
- The mixed language output must remain **organized, readable, and intentional**, not random code-switching.
- Include **relevant topic-related images, reference visuals, or source-faithful picture elements** whenever the lecture topic naturally benefits from them.
- Any image used must be **directly related to the source topic**, academically appropriate, and visually supportive rather than random decoration.
- Do **not** add unrelated stock images or visuals that are not connected to the lecture content.

## Language Style Rules for the Mixed Output

Use these style rules inside the final notes:

- Keep **main academic terms in English**.
- Explain the meaning directly after them in **Egyptian Arabic**.
- Prefer patterns like:
  - `Diffusion = يعني انتشار الجزيئات من مكان تركيزه عالي لمكان تركيزه أقل`
  - `Active Transport = يعني الخلية بتنقل المواد بالعافية وبتستهلك energy`
  - `Mitosis = يعني انقسام الخلية عشان يطلع خليتين شبه بعض`
- If a sentence must include both languages, make it feel natural and study-oriented.
- Do not turn the page into full formal Arabic.
- Do not turn the page into English-only text.
- Use Egyptian Arabic that is simple, educational, and easy for revision.
- Keep formulas, symbols, units, and scientific names untouched when needed.


## Image Integration Rules

Use these rules across all prompt variants when the topic supports visuals:

- Include **relevant images, reference illustrations, labeled topic visuals, or picture-like educational inserts** related to the lecture topic.
- If the lecture discusses **organs, cells, animals, plants, lab tools, chemicals, historical figures, places, maps, mechanisms, apparatus, charts, or physical structures**, include visuals that match those exact concepts.
- Prefer visuals that look **embedded into the handwritten notebook style**, such as pasted mini study pictures, sketched reference images, labeled cutout-style visuals, or lightly integrated infographic illustrations.
- Keep the visuals **source-faithful and academically relevant**.
- Use images to improve understanding, not just decoration.
- If the source does not support a certain image, do not invent unrelated visuals.
- Place images cleanly in the middle, right side, or corner note areas so they support the notes without overcrowding the page.
- Add short labels, arrows, or callouts connecting the image to the related English term and Egyptian Arabic explanation.
- If possible, combine both:
  - **hand-sketched diagrams**
  - **realistic topic-related study images**
- The final look should feel like **premium handwritten notes enhanced with study visuals**, not a random collage.

## Master Prompt — Balanced Premium Mixed Version

```text
Transform the provided English lecture or source file into a handwritten-style educational infographic using a structured mixed language style by following these guidelines:

1. Page Layout and Visual Appearance
- Background: Present the material on A4 notebook paper with horizontal lines, similar to a student's handwritten study notebook.
- Handwritten Style: Use a handwriting-like font that feels natural and slightly imperfect so it resembles genuine handwriting rather than a perfectly clean digital font.
- Organization: Arrange the information in clear, brief bullet points with sufficient spacing to make the notes easy to read and review quickly.

2. Mixed Language Output Style
- Keep technical terms, scientific vocabulary, formulas, topic names, and important lecture terminology in English.
- Explain each important term or idea in simple, clear Egyptian Arabic colloquial style.
- The result must feel like organized bilingual study notes, not chaotic mixing.
- Preserve the original academic meaning exactly.
- Use Egyptian Arabic for simplification, explanation, clarification, memory hints, and exam guidance.

3. Color-Based Organization
- Blue Pen: Use this color for explanations, sequential steps, and general descriptive information.
- Red Marker (Bold): Use for main titles, section headings, and official definitions.
- Yellow Highlighter: Highlight essential terms, significant dates, formulas, and final results or answers.

4. Visual Elements
- Hand-Sketched Illustrations: Add simple diagrams, charts, maps, or flow diagrams placed in the middle or toward the right side of the page. Label them clearly and connect ideas with arrows where needed.
- Topic-Related Images: Include relevant educational images, reference visuals, or realistic picture elements directly connected to the lecture topic whenever appropriate.
- Image Placement: Blend those visuals naturally into the notebook page as pasted study references, labeled inserts, or integrated infographic picture elements.
- Manual Emphasis: Draw rough circles, underlines, or marks by hand around key concepts to emphasize them.
- Margins: Decorate the margins with small hand-drawn doodles or sketches to give the notes a realistic and personalized notebook appearance.

5. Exam-Oriented Notes
- Important Indicators: Clearly mark content that is especially important for exams.
- Helpful Side Notes: Include small note boxes or bubbles labeled with phrases such as “Exam Tip,” “Common Error,” or “Memory Hint,” while the actual explanation inside can be in Egyptian Arabic.

6. Accuracy and Content Reliability
- Source Fidelity: Ensure that all information remains completely accurate and strictly derived from the uploaded file.
- No Changes to Content: Do not omit essential topics and do not add any information that does not appear in the original source.
- Keep English technical terms faithful to the wording used in the source whenever possible.

Final Result
- The finished product should resemble high-quality scanned pages of handwritten study notes, designed specifically to help with reviewing and exam preparation.
- The final output should be a clean, organized mix of English technical wording and Egyptian Arabic explanation.
```
