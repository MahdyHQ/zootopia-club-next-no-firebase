## Core Non-Negotiable Rules

Use these rules in every version:

- The source file may be in **English**, **Arabic**, or mixed language.
- The final output must be **Arabic-dominant**.
- **Most of the page content must be in Arabic**, especially the explanation, commentary, revision help, and note bubbles.
- **English should appear only where academically useful or source-faithful**, such as:
  - technical terms
  - formulas
  - laws
  - exact definitions from the lecture
  - scientific names
  - units
  - abbreviations
- Do **not** turn the page into English-heavy notes.
- Do **not** turn the page into overly formal classical Arabic if that makes it harder to revise quickly.
- Prefer **clear Egyptian educational Arabic**, especially in explanations.
- All information must remain **strictly faithful to the uploaded file**.
- Do **not** omit essential ideas.
- Do **not** add facts not present in the source.
- The result must look like **real handwritten study notes**.
- The layout must support **fast review and exam preparation**.
- The handwriting style must remain **beautiful, readable, and human-like**.

## Language Balance Rules

The model should follow this language balance:

### Arabic must dominate in:
- explanations
- simplifications
- note bubbles
- memory hints
- side comments
- revision guidance
- comparisons
- steps explained in natural language
- exam warnings
- study summaries

### English may remain in:
- formulas
- laws
- scientific terms
- exact topic names if lecture uses them that way
- abbreviations such as ATP, DNA, pH, ECG
- exact definitions when useful
- labels that must remain academically recognizable

### Recommended pattern

Use mixed lines like these:

- `Diffusion: يعني انتقال الجزيئات من منطقة تركيز عالي إلى منطقة تركيز أقل.`
- `Mitosis: يعني انقسام الخلية لإنتاج خليتين متماثلتين.`
- `Ohm's Law: القانون ده بيربط بين V و I و R، وبيوضح العلاقة بينهم بشكل مباشر.`
- `Cortisol: هرمون مهم جدًا في stress response، ووجوده هنا لازم يتفهم من سياق المحاضرة.`

### Important style guidance

- Keep the English short and purposeful.
- Let the Arabic carry the **main explanation weight**.
- The final page should feel like **Arabic handwritten notes with selected English academic anchors**, not a 50/50 mix.

## Master Prompt — Arabic-Dominant Balanced Version

```text
Transform the provided lecture or source file into a handwritten-style educational infographic using an Arabic-dominant mixed language style by following these guidelines:

1. Page Layout and Visual Appearance
- Background: Present the material on A4 notebook paper with horizontal lines, similar to a student's handwritten study notebook.
- Handwritten Style: Use a handwriting-like style that feels natural, beautiful, readable, and slightly imperfect so it resembles genuine human handwriting rather than rigid digital text.
- Organization: Arrange the information in clear, brief bullet points with enough spacing to make the notes easy to review quickly.

2. Arabic-Dominant Output Style
- Make Arabic the dominant language across the page.
- Most explanations, clarifications, study notes, memory hints, and revision guidance must be written in clear Arabic with a natural Egyptian educational tone.
- English is still allowed where academically useful, especially for scientific terms, formulas, laws, abbreviations, exact lecture terminology, and source-critical labels.
- Keep the result organized and intentional, not random code-switching.
- Do not let English dominate the page.

3. Egyptian Educational Tone
- Let the explanatory Arabic feel natural, clear, and close to Egyptian study language.
- Keep it easy to revise, not overly formal or stiff.
- Make the page feel like smart handwritten notes written by a strong Egyptian student explaining the lecture to themselves.

4. Color-Based Organization
- Blue Pen: explanations, ordered steps, descriptive content, and clarifications.
- Red Marker (Bold): main titles, definitions, laws, section headers, and warning points.
- Yellow Highlighter: essential terms, formulas, final answers, key facts, and repeated exam ideas.

5. Visual Elements
- Add hand-sketched diagrams, arrows, process flows, mini charts, or concept maps wherever supported by the source.
- Place visuals mainly in the middle or toward the right side of the page.
- Add rough circles, underlines, and emphasis marks around important concepts.
- Decorate margins lightly with small hand-drawn academic doodles.

6. Exam-Oriented Notes
- Clearly mark exam-important content.
- Include note bubbles or side boxes such as: Exam Tip, Common Error, Memory Hint.
- The label may stay in English if useful, but the inside explanation should mainly be in Arabic.

7. Accuracy and Source Fidelity
- Keep all information completely accurate and strictly derived from the uploaded file.
- Do not omit essential topics.
- Do not add information not present in the source.
- Preserve English terminology exactly when keeping it is important for academic accuracy.

8. Handwriting / Script Preference
- Make the handwriting style beautiful, readable, and close to a real person's careful notes.
- Prefer a readable Arabic handwritten script rather than decorative unreadable calligraphy.

Final Result
- The finished product should resemble premium scanned handwritten study notes.
- The page should be Arabic-dominant, exam-friendly, and visually elegant.
- Most of the explanation should be in Arabic, while selected terms, laws, and formulas may remain in English exactly where useful.
```
